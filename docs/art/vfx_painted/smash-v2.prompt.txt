A game VFX FLIPBOOK SPRITE SHEET, 1536x1024, exactly 3 columns x 2 rows of square 512x512 cells, frames read left to right, top row then bottom row (frame 1 top-left, frame 6 bottom-right). NO grid lines, NO borders, NO frame numbers: the cells are invisible, only implied by where each drawing sits.

STYLE: hand-painted digital painting, illustrated, like the effects of a top-down action game in the style of Hades II: visible brush strokes, dry-brush edges, simple bold readable shapes, a soft broken painterly edge (not a vector line, not clean geometry). Each shape is a flat fill of one tone with one inner light, like a stroke of thick paint, the same painter's hand as a hand-painted fantasy dungeon. Not a 3D render, not a photo, not pixel art, not anime, no plastic shine, no lens flare, no camera blur, no soft particle fog or airbrush glow.

COLOUR: the effect is painted in WARM GREYSCALE only (ivory to light ochre-grey, a near-white ivory core, no saturated colour at all), because a shader tints it later. BACKGROUND: pure flat black #000000 everywhere outside the strokes, no texture, no vignette, no gradient, no ground, no shadows.

REGISTRATION (very important): every cell uses the same pivot at the exact CENTRE of the cell, the same scale, the same orientation. Seen from straight above, flat on the ground (no perspective). Nothing touches or crosses a cell edge; keep about 26 px of black margin inside each cell.

FORBIDDEN: no sword, no hand, no character, no creature, no weapon; no text, letters, numbers, watermark, frame or grid; no many-pointed star, no sun with rays, no cross or any shape with four equal arms; no lion or any symbol or emblem inside the effect; no violet, no green, no red, no blood.

THE EFFECT: a HEAVY overhead smash slash seen from above, 6 frames: the shortest and thickest of all slashes. Shape: a short, very thick wedge of 120 degrees around the cell centre (from 30 degrees to 150 degrees, i.e. centred on 12 o'clock, pointing up), almost a full fan: it is thick from about 45% of the radius out to the outer radius at 90% of half the cell (about 230 px from the centre), with a heavy blunt leading end at the top-left. Outer edge clean thick brush edge, inner edge ragged dry-brush, the cell centre stays black.
Progression, same wedge, same place, same outer radius:
frame 1: full heavy fan smear, brightest and most solid;
frame 2: same, edges crisper;
frame 3: the fan slams down: the outer edge starts to crack into brush chunks;
frame 4: the fan breaks into thick broken brush chunks;
frame 5: a few chunks and streaks remain;
frame 6: two or three fading flecks.

CORRECTIONS: the wedge is a sector of a PERFECT CIRCLE centred on the cell centre, seen exactly from above, not tilted. The LOWER HALF of every cell (below the horizontal line through the centre) stays empty black. In EVERY frame the outer edge lies on the SAME circle (radius 230 px around the cell centre). Frames differ only by the paint breaking up, never by moving, rotating, growing or shrinking. It must look much heavier, thicker and shorter than a normal slash: a solid fan of paint, not a thin crescent.
MORE CORRECTIONS (a try came out wrong): the fan must NEVER break into radial stripes or rays fanning out from the centre (that reads as a sun with rays, which is forbidden). When it breaks up (frames 3 to 6), it breaks into CURVED brush streaks and chunks that run ALONG the arc, parallel to the outer edge, like thick paint dragged sideways and wearing away, the outer rim staying longest. Keep the dry-brush hand-painted texture everywhere, no blocky or pixel-like erosion. The fan is placed with its pivot at the cell centre: its inner ragged edge about 100 px above the centre, its outer edge 230 px from the centre.
